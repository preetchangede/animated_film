# The Missing Drip

Folded-paper theatre • 120-second assembly • 24 fps • 16:9 • no spoken dialogue

An old man remembers a tune his house used to play. His grandson discovers that Granddad repaired one of the instruments last summer. By the last whistle they have invented a game only the two of them know.

## The sound edit

Use one warm, breathy whistle and one coherent room tone. The vessels play left, centre, right. Establish the contour by ear in the opening whistle. A useful rehearsal pulse is 80 bpm: low enamel on beat one, darker pot on two, an exposed beat three for the high cup, whistle answering on four. Let real rain wander between these recognizable patterns. On the last pass Granddad borrows beat three and the boy answers him. Preserve the breath and ceramic decay, even across a picture cut. No underscoring under the joke.

## Continuity

P00 is the selected art direction. P01 and P02 specify the actors; P03 specifies set layout, practical light and vessel state. Use the nearest reference view for each shot. Clip labels and reference versions must travel together. Keep original image masters and every accepted take on Drive. Keep model-sheet multi-view layouts out of first-frame inputs; compose a single cinematic frame first. Stage the main camera on the room side of the boy–Granddad line: boy left; Granddad right. Window blue hits paper edges; a faceted lamp from the right warms cheek and collar. Chipped blue cup stays dry until shot 12. Its handle points right in the master angle. Teapot and pot are open to the leaks. New ceiling patch is dry. Rust scarf belongs on the chair. Boy's curls and hood retain their shapes; movement can be brisk at the eyes and slow at the shoulders. Keep card thickness and contact shadows visible. No rubbery limb stretch. Keep the waves small and far away.
